What I actually believe about this work

If you cannot measure it, you do not get to claim it. A lot of what gets called CRO is redecoration delivered in a confident voice.

Structure is the design decision, styling is the consequence

The work that decides whether an interface holds up is done before anything looks like anything: what the hierarchy is, what states exist, what happens when the data does not arrive. On the largest system I have designed, the defining decision came from a component being 240 pixels wide across all 488 of its variants. On a rebrand, it came from resolving the whole structure in low fidelity so nobody could argue about colour while the sequence was still unsettled.

I would rather hand over a plain looking thing that is correct than a beautiful one that invents its own edge cases during build.

Measurement comes before design, not after

The most common thing I inherit is an account where the tracking is wrong and nobody knows. Form starts firing on page view. Goals counting the wrong thing. A dashboard everybody trusts that is quietly describing a different website. Redesigning on top of that is not optimisation, it is guessing with extra steps.

So on a new account I audit and rebuild the instrumentation, then collect sixty to ninety days of clean data before I write a single hypothesis.

A blended number is usually hiding the answer

On one account the form completion rate read 11.6 percent, which looked like a broken form. Segmented to the client's actual target customer it was 35 percent, which is a different problem entirely: the form was working, the traffic was wrong. Nobody had segmented it. That single cut of the data redirected the whole roadmap away from redesigning a form that did not need redesigning.

Constraints produce better decisions than freedom does

The best product work I have done was inside a platform that would not let me write a line of CSS. Having no room to invent my way out of a problem meant every choice had to be justified against something real, in that case the actual pixel width of the components available. I did not enjoy it at the time. The output was better than it would have been with a blank canvas.

Failure states are the design

Most design work ships the happy path and leaves engineering to invent the error states. That is backwards. The error states are where a user decides whether to trust a tool, and a tool nobody trusts does not get used regardless of how good the main flow looks.

Disagreeing with the client is part of the job

On one engagement I wrote a document structured as the client's valid concern, then the strategic counter point, then the recommendation, for each of three areas where we did not agree. Framing it that way meant nobody had to lose. It is the piece of work I would point at if you asked me what changed between being a designer and being a consultant.

A number you cannot defend is worse than no number

I would rather present one result I can walk through end to end, including what else changed at the same time and which part of the improvement was measurement correction rather than behaviour change, than three impressive figures I would stumble on under questioning. Being caught inflating is not recoverable.

Where I am honest about the gaps

I would rather you heard these from me than found them in an interview.

  • Statistical rigour. I have run A/B tests in VWO and Convert and read the significance the platforms report, but I have not run my own power calculations. On the traffic volumes I usually work with most tests would be underpowered anyway, which is part of why I lean on properly instrumented before and after measurement over sixty to ninety days rather than pretending a two week test on thin traffic told us something. I am closing this gap deliberately.
  • Primary user research. I have not run interview or survey programmes. I work from behavioural data, session recordings and secondary research into the vertical and business model, triangulated against heuristic findings. That is real work, but it is secondary research and I call it that. It is the capability I most want to add next.
  • Mentoring. I have been the only person in my discipline on the team, so I have not managed or mentored anyone directly. What I did instead was build the processes, guidelines and templates the function runs on, and those were picked up by squads beyond my own. I would like a role where the next step is people rather than only process.
